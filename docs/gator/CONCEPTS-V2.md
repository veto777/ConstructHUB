# Gator shorts — the fail slate (V2)

The owner, 2026-10-08: *"We need some viral clips like someone getting injured on a job site or a house getting
flooded or collapsing, or someone falling off a ladder! Or a goat yelling! We need those hooks! … Your comedy
isn't great. Find some obvious construction clichés and use them."* The first thirty concepts
(`CONCEPTS.md`) were gentle and observational. This slate is fail-video energy: a picture that stops a thumb
in the first second.

## What actually gets shared (research, 2026-10-08)

1. **The hook is a picture, not a sentence.** The clips the owner sent — an AI squirrel "doing parkour" over an
   AC unit (459k likes), an AI selfie vlog that falls apart while its character keeps talking, a cat that
   knocks out a slap fighter (1.8M likes), a kicker who goes flat on his back (10M likes) — are each ONE take,
   one impossible-but-almost-believable gag, understood with the sound off, with **nothing burned into the
   picture**: no meme text, no logo, no end card. The caption does the framing.
2. **Photoreal AI animals in "found footage" are the format of the moment.** Fake security-camera and doorbell
   clips of animals reached nine-figure views in 2025 (a leopard and a cat on a porch: 120M views in a week;
   rabbits on a trampoline: 203M on TikTok — reporting by AOL / Lead Stories / Bloomberg). They are also being
   fact-checked as deceptive when unlabelled: **we label every clip AI-generated and tag #AIContent**.
3. **Trade humour is cliché recognition.** What a crew sends to a crew is the line everyone has heard: *that's
   not load-bearing · measure once, cut twice · do your best, caulk the rest · between eight and four · my
   cousin can do it cheaper · while you're here · who did this? · the board stretcher · it's a small leak ·
   one more trip · Friday two o'clock pour.* General 2026 trend round-ups say the same thing in other words:
   "personality over polish — deadpan humour, quick reveals, carefree single shots".
4. **The structure of a fail that travels:** the viewer sees the trap before the victim does (the kicker clip
   spends eight of its thirteen seconds on the set-up); the payoff is one clean fall; and he is fine. The edit
   that multiplies it is the instant replay.
5. **Talking works when the talker never stops** — the disaster interrupts a confident sentence and he finishes
   it anyway. A dry button line after someone else's disaster ("Tie off.") is the same joke from the outside.

## The comedy engine

**HOOK (0–1 s, mid-action) → a beat of denial or deadpan → BUTTON** (a second, bigger thing, or the gator's one
line). Start ON the fall, the crack, the burst — or on the confident sentence the fall will cut off.

The cast (invented; recurring): **the gator** (foreman; deadpan; has seen it all; always fine), **the rooster
apprentice** (cocky, wrong, loud), **the possum** (homeowner in a bathrobe, or the inspector who plays dead),
**the beaver framer** (competent; suffers), **the raccoons** (the crew; they nod at each other before they do
it), **the goat** (screams; that is all he does).

**THE LINE — the comedy lives inside it.** Looney-Tunes physics only: the gator or an animal takes the hit and
pops back up — dazed, dusty, hat crooked, thumbs up. No realistic person is injured (the two "giant opponent"
clips show an invented man fall into something soft and sit up laughing — that is the limit). No blood, no
bones, no lasting harm, nobody dies, no children. Nothing that reads as a real accident, a real animal attack
or cruelty; the gator never bites or threatens. Unsafe practice is never advice: the joke is always ON the
shortcut and the one who took it. No real people, brands, logos, leagues or other studios' characters. Every
clip is labelled AI-generated.

**Sound is half of it:** the take's own crash and voice (the video model renders them); in the edit a hit on
the frame of impact, a rewind scrub, a bass drop under the slow-motion replay, CC0 music
(`MUSIC-LICENCES.md`) or none — and the goat. −14 LUFS with the peaks tamed.

## How these were made

One photoreal model sheet of the gator (text-to-image), a still per scene drawn from it, one take per scene on
a video model with its own sound; dialogue written in the prompt and checked afterwards by a local
speech-to-text; the goat generated once and cut in; replays and music in the edit. See `STYLES.md` for the
recipe and cost of each style, `VOICE.md` for the voice, `SCOREBOARD.md` for what the numbers say once the
clips are out.
